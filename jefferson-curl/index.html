The Jefferson curl, measured

The Jefferson curl is a loaded spinal flexion exercise: a standing lifter holds a weight and flexes the spine sequentially from the neck downward, lowering the weight toward the feet.

By Tomer Yona, PhD, physiotherapist and biomechanics researcher

Muscle activity at the same load

Muscle activity was higher in the Jefferson curl than in the Romanian deadlift at three of five sites.

The study is the first surface EMG characterization of the Jefferson curl. Its 21 resistance-trained adults performed both lifts with the same absolute load (mean 41.9 kg), set in the Jefferson curl at a Borg rating of perceived exertion of 16.

Not measured: load on the spine, injury risk, flexibility, training outcomes.

Questions

What muscles does the Jefferson curl work?

The study measured five sites. Muscle activity in the erector spinae (along the spine) and the biceps femoris (a hamstring) was 52% to 63% of maximal voluntary isometric contraction; in the gluteus maximus, 31%.

Jefferson curl vs Romanian deadlift (RDL): what is the difference?

The Jefferson curl flexes the spine. The Romanian deadlift keeps the spine neutral, with the knees slightly bent and the hips pushed back. At the same load, muscle activity was higher in the Jefferson curl at three of five sites.

How do you do a Jefferson curl?

In the study, lifters stood barefoot on a 30 cm platform holding a barbell in front of the thighs. They flexed the spine in a controlled sequence from the neck downward to their maximum comfortable flexion, then returned to standing.

Is the Jefferson curl safe, or bad for your back?

The study cannot say. It measured muscle activity, not load on the spine or injury risk.

What are the benefits of the Jefferson curl?

The authors conclude that the Jefferson curl can be considered for targeted erector spinae and hamstring recruitment through controlled spinal flexion. Effects on strength, flexibility and pain were not measured.
